Evidence 1 · Research and information literacy · Capstone, Spring 2026

Research: how companies move to the cloud

Many large companies keep some of their systems on their own servers and some with one or more cloud providers, such as AWS and Microsoft Azure. Using a mix of your own servers and the cloud is called "hybrid cloud"; using more than one provider is called "multicloud." For my senior capstone, I researched how companies move their data and systems between these environments without things going wrong.

The question

Moving data between your own servers and different cloud providers can run into systems that don't fit together, migrations that take longer than planned, and costly downtime when services go offline.

How I researched it

I reviewed guidance from NIST (the U.S. standards agency), AWS, Microsoft Azure and IBM, along with academic articles. I compared the strategies they recommend, including AWS's "7 Rs," a framework of seven ways to move an application to the cloud.

What I found

The biggest risk isn't hackers, it's downtime. Successful moves map out which systems depend on each other, schedule downtime in advance, keep backup servers running in stages, and have a plan to roll back if something fails.

Evidence 3 · Teamwork and leadership · Marymount IT department, Fall 2026

Co-leading a team of interns

During my internship I co-led the rebuild of a 24-Mac computer lab. About four interns contributed over six weeks, and another intern and I did most of the work and kept it moving.

Leading the work

Shared leadership of the project from start to finish: setting up and tracking the new Macs, retiring and wiping the old ones, and making the lab manageable remotely.

Teaching others

Trained other interns on the procedures for the projects I worked on, and wrote setup guides so the work could be repeated after me.

The result

The lab runs on new hardware, and the IT office can update and support every machine from their desks.
